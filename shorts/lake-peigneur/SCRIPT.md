# The Lake That Went Down the Drain

## Facts (with sources)
- 20 Nov 1980, Lake Peigneur, Louisiana. A Texaco-contracted rig's 14-inch drill stuck at 1,228 ft, then pierced the
  Diamond Crystal salt mine under the lake. Fresh water dissolved the salt and widened the hole; the lake drained in
  about 3 hours. (Wikipedia: Lake Peigneur; 64 Parishes; Gizmodo "How a 14-inch hole created...")
- Lake was shallow: max ~10-11 ft. (Wikipedia; 64 Parishes)
- Swallowed: the drilling platform, 11 barges, a tugboat, trees and part of Jefferson Island. (Wikipedia)
- The Delcambre Canal flowed backwards, feeding Gulf water into the hole; a waterfall formed (height reported as 50 or
  150 ft, so no number is used). The lake refilled in about 2 days. (Wikipedia; Atlas Obscura; 64 Parishes)
- More than 50 miners got out; nobody died. (64 Parishes; UPI via search)
- Leonce Viator Jr. was fishing on the lake with his nephew that morning and got off safely. (Wikipedia)
- Several sunken barges later popped back up (count not confirmed, so none is used). (Wikipedia)
- The lake went from fresh to salt water and is much deeper. (Wikipedia; Gizmodo)
- Cause was never proven (wrong map or wrong drill spot). (MSHA 1981 via Wikipedia)

## Script
```
In 1980, an oil crew in Louisiana drilled one small hole, and an entire lake went down the drain.
Lake Peigneur was calm and shallow, barely ten feet deep. Perfect for fishing. And far below it, miners were digging salt.
One November morning, a drilling rig is out on the water, hunting for oil. Twelve hundred feet down, the drill gets stuck.
Then the whole rig starts to tilt. The crew jumps for it.
Out on the lake, a man fishing with his nephew notices something odd. His boat is moving. Toward the middle.
The water starts to spin. A whirlpool opens up and swallows the rig. Then a tugboat. Then eleven barges, one after another.
Then the trees. Then part of an island.
Three hours later, the lake is gone.
Luckily, every miner below got out in time.
Unluckily, the lake was now in their mine.
You see, that mine was carved out of solid salt, and the drill had poked a hole right into its roof.
Ever dropped a sugar cube into hot tea? Water eats salt the same way.
The hole got wider, so more water rushed in, so the hole got even wider.
Then the canal that used to drain the lake into the sea ran backwards, and made a brand new waterfall.
Two days later, the lake was full again.
And several of the barges popped back up, like bath toys.
The lake was back. It was just saltier. And a whole lot deeper.
```
